What it is

The Minimoog Voyager, released in 2002, was Bob Moog's modern reimagining of the Model D — the instrument that reintroduced Moog as a going concern. It keeps the three-oscillator, fat-filtered Minimoog spirit but adds programmability, MIDI, an expressive touch pad, and patchable expansion, wrapped in a signature backlit panel. Produced in several editions (the standard, the Select Series, the Old School, and the smaller Voyager RME) until it was discontinued, it's a modern classic in its own right.

Buying one used — what to check

The Voyager is a robust modern instrument, so it's lower-risk than a vintage Minimoog, but there are specifics to check. The backlit panel and the touch surface are its signature features — confirm both work — and test the wheels, the pots, and that all three oscillators tune and track cleanly. Know the editions: the standard, the various Select Series and anniversary finishes, the Old School (no patch memory or MIDI, deliberately), and the compact Voyager RME differ in features and value, so read the title carefully. Ask about firmware and any servicing.

Regional sourcing notes

The Voyager is American-built, so it appears in Japan less often than home-grown gear, but it does surface on Yahoo Auctions and through Japanese dealers. Voltage runs both ways — a US unit is 117V and a Japanese-market unit is 100V — so confirm which you're buying. In Japanese listings look for モーグ or ムーグ (Moog) and ボイジャー (Voyager); the condition vocabulary is the same — 動作確認済み (tested working), 整備済み (serviced), 美品 (excellent), against ジャンク (junk) and 未確認 (untested).
