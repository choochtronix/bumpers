What it is

The Roland SP-404, first released in 2005, is the battery-capable portable sampler that became a lo-fi and hip-hop staple. Sample onto its pads, mangle with its onboard effects — the vinyl sim, the resonant filters, the cassette-style warmth — and perform or resample on the fly; that immediacy and its gritty character built a devoted following. The line has grown through the SP-404SX, the SP-404A, and the current SP-404MKII, which added a deeper effects engine and modern connectivity while keeping the core workflow.

Buying one used — what to check

This is a modern, robust sampler, so buying used is lower-risk than a vintage instrument, but which version you're getting matters most. The original SP-404, the SP-404SX, the SP-404A, and the SP-404MKII differ meaningfully in features, effects, and value, and prices vary accordingly — read the exact model in the title, not just SP-404. On any unit, check the pads for even response and wear, the encoders and buttons, the screen, and whether it ships with the original power supply (the older units use a specific adapter). For the older SX and A, confirm the SD card slot reads reliably; for the MKII, confirm the firmware is current.

Regional sourcing notes

As a current Roland product the SP-404 line is everywhere in Japan and often the best-value place to buy — Yahoo Auctions, Mercari, and Hard Off list every generation, frequently cheaper than Western retail even accounting for shipping. Domestic units are 100V, though the external adapters are usually easy to substitute. In Japanese listings search ローランド (Roland) and SP-404, SP-404SX, or SP-404MK2, and read condition terms — 動作確認済み (tested working), 整備済み (serviced), 美品 (excellent), against ジャンク (junk) and 未確認 (untested).
